# References

An annotated bibliography of the theory this package builds on. Derivations that used to live here (the
grade-basis size, the DLA truncation floor, the relaxation reduction ladder) have moved into the guides
([Grade Truncation and Polynomial State Space Scaling](@ref), [Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism](@ref), and
[Relaxation Backends](@ref)) and are cross-referenced below.

## Restricted State Space and Truncation

[Kuprov, Wagner-Rundell, Hore](@id ref-kuprov-2007), "Polynomially scaling spin dynamics simulation algorithm based on adaptive
state-space restriction," J. Magn. Reson. 189, 241 (2007). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmr.2007.09.014.
The origin of the restricted-state-space idea this package implements: keep only low-order product
operators and the cost scales polynomially instead of as `4^N`. The Spinach lineage. Their exactness axiom
is ours: the retained set must be closed under the generator (their "loose ends" leaving the space are the
boundary block `Q L P` of [Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis](@ref)).

[Kuprov](@id ref-kuprov-2008), "Polynomially scaling spin dynamics II: further state-space compression using Krylov subspace
techniques and zero track elimination," J. Magn. Reson. 195, 45 (2008).
https://spindynamics.org/publications/016_kuprov_2008.pdf. Zero track elimination (ZTE): probe a trajectory,
drop density-matrix rows ("tracks") that stay near zero, and propagate on the reduced Liouvillian. The direct
ancestor of [`zero_track_elimination`](@ref). The observation that the trajectory lives in the Krylov subspace
`span{rho0, L rho0, L^2 rho0, ...}` is what [`reachable_basis`](@ref) enumerates symbolically on the coupling
graph instead of numerically by Lanczos.

[Krzystyniak, Edwards, Kuprov](@id ref-krzystyniak-2011), "Destination state screening of active spaces in spin dynamics simulations,"
J. Magn. Reson. 210, 228 (2011). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmr.2011.01.005. Screens the active space by which
states connect backward to the detected (destination) states, the observable-informed pruning behind
`direction = :observable` and the `criterion = :dual` importance weight in [`zero_track_elimination`](@ref).

[Karabanov, Kuprov, Charnock, van der Drift, Edwards, Kockenberger](@id ref-karabanov-2011), "On the accuracy of the state space
restriction approximation for spin dynamics simulations," J. Chem. Phys. 135, 084106 (2011).
https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3866. The accuracy theory for SSR: correlation order climbs the ladder at a bounded
speed set by the coupling `||H_2||` and is drained by relaxation `||R_1||`, so leakage past a cutoff grade is
bounded by the ratio `||H_2|| / ||R_1||`. Their per-order norm `|rho_k|^2` is the statistical counterpart of
the trajectory-weighted flow meter in [Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis](@ref); the exact algebraic version is the
[`dla_report`](@ref) grade floor.

[Kuprov](@id ref-kuprov-2023), *Spin: From Basic Symmetries to Quantum Optimal Control* (Springer, 2023).
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-05607-9. Frames spin dynamics in controllability / reachability /
observability terms and develops GRAPE optimal control, the same system-theoretic language used here for
basis selection in [Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis](@ref), applied there to pulse design.

Wiersema, Kokcu, Kemper, Bakalov, "Classification of dynamical Lie algebras of 2-local spin systems on
linear, circular and fully connected topologies," npj Quantum Information 10, 110 (2024).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-024-00900-2. The formal backing for the truncation floor: grade truncation
is exact iff the dynamical Lie algebra closes within the kept grades, and the DLA (hence the required grade)
is set by the coupling topology. See [Grade Truncation and Polynomial State Space Scaling](@ref).

Kokcu, Wiersema, Kemper, Bakalov, "Classification of dynamical Lie algebras generated by spin interactions on
undirected graphs," J. Math. Phys. 67, 052205 (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19797. Extends the DLA
classification from chains, rings, and complete graphs to arbitrary undirected coupling graphs: beyond one
dimension the DLA depends only on whether the graph is bipartite, and a polynomially-sized DLA (hence a low,
spin-count-independent [`dla_report`](@ref) grade floor) is special to 1D. The topological answer to when
grade truncation stays cheap: chain-like coupling closes at low grade, while dense or higher-dimensional
graphs generically do not. See [Grade Truncation and Polynomial State Space Scaling](@ref).

## Open-System Relaxation Theory

Kubo, "Stochastic Liouville Equations," J. Math. Phys. 4, 174 (1963); Anderson, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 9, 316
(1954). The exact classical-bath route implemented by [`sle`](@ref) / [`propagate_sle`](@ref): a
Hamiltonian fluctuating over a classical M-state bath, valid at any field and through level crossings. The
top of the reduction ladder in [Relaxation Backends](@ref).

Gorini, Kossakowski, Sudarshan, J. Math. Phys. 17, 821 (1976); Lindblad, Commun. Math. Phys. 48, 119
(1976). The general completely-positive (GKSL) generator with a PSD Kossakowski matrix. Off-diagonal
entries are cross-correlated relaxation (CSA x dipolar, the basis of TROSY and a driver of singlet-order
leakage in the Pileio-Levitt long-lived-states theory); assembled here by [`kossakowski_matrix`](@ref) and
[`dissipators_from_kossakowski`](@ref).

Davidovic, "Completely Positive, Simple, and Possibly Highly Accurate Approximation of the Redfield
Equation," Quantum 4, 326 (2020). https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2020-09-21-326. The geometric-mean master
equation restores complete positivity to Bloch-Redfield by replacing an arithmetic mean of decay rates
with a geometric one, keeping non-secular accuracy. The `method = :davidovic` path of [`redfield_jumps`](@ref).

Nathan, Rudner, "Universal Lindblad equation for open quantum systems," Phys. Rev. B 102, 115109 (2020).
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.102.115109. Recasts Bloch-Redfield into Lindblad form with jump operators
filtered by `sqrt(g(omega))`, completely positive by construction and handling quasi-degeneracy. The
`method = :nathan_rudner` path.

Levitt, Di Bari, "Steady state in magnetic resonance pulse experiments," Phys. Rev. Lett. 69, 3124 (1992).
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.69.3124 (and the follow-up "The homogeneous master equation and the
manipulation of relaxation networks," Bull. Magn. Reson., 1994). The homogeneous master equation: a single
inhomogeneous term drives relaxation toward thermal equilibrium, the detailed-balance foundation the later
thermalized Lindblad forms build on.

[Bengs, Levitt](@id ref-bengs-levitt), "A master equation for spin systems far from equilibrium," J. Magn. Reson. 310, 106645
(2020). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmr.2019.106645. The thermalized Lindblad ("Lb") master equation: the
secular GKSL dissipator with the classical spectral density weighted by the Schofield / KMS factor
`exp(-beta*omega/2)`, giving a completely positive generator that relaxes to the Boltzmann state even far
from equilibrium (unlike the inhomogeneous master equation). The basis for [`bengs_levitt_jumps`](@ref),
[`davies_jumps`](@ref), and the `thermalize` flag of the Zeeman-secular backends.

Schofield, "Space-Time Correlation Function Formalism for Slow Neutron Scattering," Phys. Rev. Lett. 4,
239 (1960). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.4.239. Origin of the quantum detailed-balance (Schofield)
correction `exp(-beta*omega/2)` that asymmetrizes the classical spectral density so the thermalized
generators above relax to the Boltzmann state rather than infinite temperature.

Schaller, Brandes, "Preservation of positivity by dynamical coarse graining," Phys. Rev. A 78, 022106
(2008); Trushechkin, "Unified GKLS quantum master equation beyond the secular approximation," Phys. Rev. A
103, 062226 (2021). Two further CP-preserving, non-secular reconstructions that interpolate between Redfield
and the secular limit, context for the CP-vs-accuracy tradeoff discussed in [Relaxation Backends](@ref).

## Paramagnetic and Second-Kind Relaxation

Abragam, *The Principles of Nuclear Magnetism* (Oxford, 1961), ch. VIII; Solomon, Phys. Rev. 99, 559
(1955); Bloembergen, J. Chem. Phys. 27, 572 (1957). Relaxation of the first and second kind, and the
dipolar cross/auto (Solomon) rates. The second-kind picture, a fast-relaxing partner eliminated into a
fluctuating field, is [`ScalarSecondKind`](@ref) / [`DipolarSecondKind`](@ref), and the
[`solomon_rates`](@ref) the first-kind dipolar backend reproduces.

Hwang, Freed, "Dynamic effects of pair correlation functions on spin relaxation by translational diffusion
in liquids," J. Chem. Phys. 63, 4017 (1975); Freed, ibid. 68, 4034 (1978); Ayant, Belorizky, Fries,
Rosset, J. Physique 36, 991 (1975). The force-free translational spectral density for a freely diffusing
partner, with its characteristic `sqrt(omega)` low-frequency dispersion, implemented as
[`Translational`](@ref) and the physics behind the outer-sphere paramagnetic channel.

Caravan, Ellison, McMurry, Lauffer, "Gadolinium(III) chelates as MRI contrast agents," Chem. Rev. 99, 2293
(1999). The outer-sphere relaxivity lineage; [`outer_sphere_second_kind`](@ref) / [`outer_sphere_r1`](@ref)
package the freely-diffusing case as a per-mM relaxivity, validated against the TEMPOL nitroxide NMRD
profile across roughly eight decades of field (`scripts/validation/validate_nitroxide_nmrd.jl`).

## Electronic-Structure Inputs

[Neese](@id ref-orca), "The ORCA program system," WIREs Comput. Mol. Sci. 2, 73 (2012).
https://doi.org/10.1002/wcms.81; Neese, Wennmohs, Becker, Riplinger, "The ORCA quantum chemistry program
package," J. Chem. Phys. 152, 224108 (2020). https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0004608. The DFT engine for the
first-principles spin parameters used here: the scalar-coupling (SSCC) networks and GIAO isotropic shieldings
behind the strychnine 2D NOESY J-network and ¹H shifts in [Benchmarks](benchmarks.md) and the DFT-derived
couplings and shifts in [Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis](@ref).

## SABRE and Chemical Exchange

[Barskiy, Knecht, Yurkovskaya, Ivanov](@id ref-barskiy), "SABRE: Chemical kinetics and spin dynamics of the
formation of hyperpolarization," Prog. Nucl. Magn. Reson. Spectrosc. 114-115, 33 (2019).
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnmrs.2019.05.005. The analytical model behind the SABRE pump channels: the
exchange reset of the parahydrogen hydride pair to the fresh singlet (`singlet_refresh`, Eq. 72) and
the reduced exchange-vs-transfer pump rate (`sabre_rate`, Eq. 68, peaking at `k_d = ω_LAC`).

[Markelov, Snadin, Kiryutin, Barskiy, Yurkovskaya](@id ref-markelov), "Scalable modeling of multi-spin ensembles in SABRE
hyperpolarization: a symmetry-based framework for zero and ultralow fields," Magn. Reson. 7, 53 (2026).
https://doi.org/10.5194/mr-7-53-2026. A contemporary symmetry-restricted approach to the problem this
package targets: at zero and ultralow field the Hamiltonian, relaxation, and exchange superoperators share a
total-spin symmetry, so the dynamics restrict rigorously to the zero-quantum coherence subspace. That yields
a scalable multi-spin SABRE simulation, validated against full Liouville space and applied to a 14-spin
complex. The zero-quantum restriction is the coherence-order (`weight`) ladder sector here (see [Basis &
Symmetry](@ref)); the exchange reset is `singlet_refresh` / `sabre`.

## Projection Methods and Reduced Dynamics

Nakajima, Prog. Theor. Phys. 20, 948 (1958); Zwanzig, J. Chem. Phys. 33, 1338 (1960); Breuer, Petruccione,
*The Theory of Open Quantum Systems* (Oxford, 2002), Ch. 9. The projection-operator formalism behind the grade
reductions: the Nakajima-Zwanzig memory kernel ([`memory_kernel`](@ref)) and its Markovian Schur-complement
limit ([`effective_liouvillian`](@ref)), the time-convolutionless generator ([`tcl_generator`](@ref)), and the
initial inhomogeneity for a populated complement. See [Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism](@ref).

Van Loan, "Computing integrals involving the matrix exponential," IEEE Trans. Autom. Control 23, 395
(1978). https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1978.1101743. The block-triangular ("augmented") exponential that
evaluates the time-convolutionless `M(t) = (e^{Q𝓛Q t} - I)(Q𝓛Q)⁻¹` in a single `exp` without inverting
`Q𝓛Q`, the numerical engine behind [`tcl_generator`](@ref).

[Shabani, Lidar](@id ref-shabani-lidar), "Completely positive post-Markovian master equation via a measurement approach," Phys. Rev. A
71, 020101(R) (2005). The post-Markovian master equation ([`pome`](@ref)): the Markovian generator convolved
with a phenomenological exponential memory kernel, embedded as a local generator on the state plus one
auxiliary memory mode.

## Symmetry Adaptation

Dixon, "High speed computation of group characters," Numer. Math. 10, 446 (1967).
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02162877. The multiplication-table route to irreducible characters behind
[`character_table`](@ref): the class sums' structure-constant matrices commute, so their common
eigenvectors carry the central characters, no cycle-type class key or stored irrep tables needed. Feeds
the multi-dimensional-irrep analysis ([`irrep_multiplicities`](@ref), [`symmetry_report`](@ref)).

## Non-normality and Transient Growth

Trefethen, Embree, *Spectra and Pseudospectra: The Behavior of Nonnormal Matrices and Operators* (Princeton,
2005); Kreiss, "Ueber die Stabilitaetsdefinition fuer Differenzengleichungen die partielle
Differentialgleichungen approximieren," BIT 2, 153 (1962). Because the restricted Liouvillian is non-normal,
its spectral abscissa governs only the long-time rate while the norm of `exp(tL)` can amplify transiently
first. The [Spectral (Contour)](@ref) transient-growth tools rest on this theory: the numerical abscissa
([`RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.numerical_abscissa`](@ref)), the Kreiss constant and its two-sided bracket
([`RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.kreiss_bracket`](@ref)), the measured envelope
([`RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.transient_bound`](@ref)), and the optimal-growth initial state
([`RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.optimal_growth`](@ref)).

[Petermann](@id ref-petermann), "Calculated spontaneous emission factor for double-heterostructure injection lasers with
gain-induced waveguiding," IEEE J. Quantum Electron. 15, 566 (1979).
https://doi.org/10.1109/JQE.1979.1070064; Siegman, "Excess spontaneous emission in non-Hermitian optical
systems. I. Laser amplifiers," Phys. Rev. A 39, 1253 (1989). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.39.1253. The
excess-noise (Petermann) factor: the norm of the non-normal spectral projector, equal to the squared
eigenvalue condition number for a simple eigenvalue and diverging at an exceptional point. Reported as the
operator norm `‖P‖₂²` (not the Frobenius norm, which differ for a degenerate cluster) by
[`RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.petermann_factor`](@ref) and
[`RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.eigstructure`](@ref).

## Fitting, Model Selection, and State Estimation

[Golub, Pereyra](@id ref-varpro), "The differentiation of pseudo-inverses and nonlinear least squares problems
whose variables separate," SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 10, 413 (1973). https://doi.org/10.1137/0710036;
[Marquardt](@id ref-lm), "An algorithm for least-squares estimation of nonlinear parameters," J. Soc. Indust.
Appl. Math. 11, 431 (1963). https://doi.org/10.1137/0111030. The nonlinear-least-squares methods behind
[`fit`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.fit): the Gauss-Newton / Levenberg-Marquardt step of the joint
structural fit, and the separable variable-projection (VARPRO) fit
([`Separable`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.Separable), which concentrates the complex amplitudes out).

[Barkhuijsen, de Beer, Bovée, van Ormondt](@id ref-hsvd), "Retrieval of frequencies, amplitudes, damping
factors, and phases from time-domain signals using a linear least-squares procedure," J. Magn. Reson. 61, 465
(1985). https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-2364(85)90187-8; Hua, Sarkar, "Matrix pencil method for estimating
parameters of exponentially damped/undamped sinusoids in noise," IEEE Trans. Acoust. Speech Signal Process.
38, 814 (1990). https://doi.org/10.1109/29.56027. The Hankel-SVD and matrix-pencil line estimators behind
[`hsvd`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.hsvd): form a Hankel matrix from the FID, take its signal
subspace from the SVD, and read the poles off the subspace shift-invariance, recovering frequencies, damping,
and amplitudes with no cost function and no starting guess. The seed-free front end that seeds
[`fit`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.fit).

[Govindaraju, Young, Maudsley](@id ref-govindaraju), "Proton NMR chemical shifts and coupling constants for
brain metabolites," NMR Biomed. 13, 129 (2000).
https://doi.org/10.1002/1099-1492(200005)13:3%3C129::AID-NBM619%3E3.0.CO;2-V. The tabulated metabolite
chemical shifts and `J`-couplings that LCModel and the related MRS basis sets are built from, the published
ground truth against which [`hsvd`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.hsvd) is validated on lactate in
[Validation](validation.md).

[Hoerl, Kennard](@id ref-ridge), "Ridge regression: biased estimation for nonorthogonal problems,"
Technometrics 12, 55 (1970). https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1970.10488634. The L2 penalty that stabilizes an
ill-conditioned least-squares estimate, equivalently a Gaussian prior on the parameters (Tikhonov
regularization). [`GaussianPrior`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.GaussianPrior) is its general form,
and [`flat_prior`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.flat_prior) ridges only the flat Fisher directions.

[Akaike](@id ref-aic), "A new look at the statistical model identification," IEEE Trans. Automat. Control 19,
716 (1974). https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1974.1100705; [Hurvich, Tsai](@id ref-aicc), "Regression and time
series model selection in small samples," Biometrika 76, 297 (1989). https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/76.2.297.
The Akaike criterion and its small-sample correction (AICc), the model-selection scores of
[`info_criteria`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.info_criteria) that penalize extra parameters.

[Rao](@id ref-crb), "Information and the accuracy attainable in the estimation of statistical parameters,"
Bull. Calcutta Math. Soc. 37, 81 (1945); Kay, *Fundamentals of Statistical Signal Processing: Estimation
Theory* (Prentice Hall, 1993), ISBN 978-0133457117; [Helstrom](@id ref-qfi), *Quantum Detection and Estimation
Theory* (Academic Press, 1976), ISBN 978-0123400505; Braunstein, Caves, "Statistical distance and the geometry
of quantum states," Phys. Rev. Lett. 72, 3439 (1994). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.72.3439. The
Cramér-Rao bound and Fisher information. [`uncertainty`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.uncertainty)
evaluates the classical Fisher `F = Jacᵀ diag(1/σ²) Jac`, the Cramér-Rao bound for the specified measurement
under Gaussian detection noise (Rao; Kay). The quantum Fisher information (Helstrom; Braunstein-Caves), the
bound optimized over all measurements through the symmetric logarithmic derivative, is the ceiling above it,
not what RSD evaluates.

[Rao, Rawlings, Mayne](@id ref-mhe), "Constrained state estimation for nonlinear discrete-time systems:
stability and moving horizon approximations," IEEE Trans. Automat. Control 48, 246 (2003).
https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2002.808470. The moving-horizon estimator:
[`smooth`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.smooth) is one window, and
[`moving_horizon`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.moving_horizon) /
[`step!`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.step!) the recursive filter, with the arrival cost carrying the
information from before the window.

[Leggett, Garg](@id ref-leggett-garg), "Quantum mechanics versus macroscopic realism: is the flux there when
nobody looks?," Phys. Rev. Lett. 54, 857 (1985). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.54.857. The Leggett-Garg
inequality that [`leggett_garg`](@ref RestrictedSpinDynamics.Inference.leggett_garg) tests: a macrorealist
bound on the three-time correlator `K3`, violated by coherent quantum evolution up to the Lüders bound.
