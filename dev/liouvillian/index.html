Assembling the Liouvillian

build_liouvillian assembles the sparse superoperator $\mathcal{L} = -i[H,\cdot] + \sum_j r_j\,D[L_j]$ in the restricted basis, applying grade projection column by column. On a sectored basis check_sector_closure verifies the dynamics stays in the sector, keeping the restriction exact.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.build_liouvillian — Method
build_liouvillian(basis, H; jumps = [], check_sector = true) -> SparseMatrixCSC

Assemble the restricted Liouvillian 𝓛 = -i[H, ·] + Σ_j r_j D[L_j] (D[L]ρ = LρL† - ½{L†L, ρ}) as a sparse superoperator on the coefficient vector, each column projected to grade ≤ kmax (the drop that makes 𝓛 non-normal). H is a Pauli dict, jumps a vector of (L::PauliDict, rate::Real); dispatch picks the representation from the basis (Pauli, ladder weight-sector, or spherical-tensor). When basis is sectored, check_sector (default true) verifies the dynamics stay in it via check_sector_closure.

Examples

julia> basis = restricted_basis(4, 2);

julia> H = hamiltonian(zeeman(4, zeros(4)), jcoupling(4, 1, 2, 5.0));

julia> L = build_liouvillian(basis, H; jumps = dissipator(decay(4, 1:4, 1.0)));

julia> size(L)          # a sparse superoperator on the coefficient vector
(67, 67)
source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.check_sector_closure — Function
check_sector_closure(basis, H, jumps)

Verify the dynamics (H + jumps) preserve basis.sector, so restricting to it stays exact. No-op for an unsectored basis; throws ArgumentError on a leak.

  • Parity sector (:even/:odd): every H term has even coherence-order parity (a transverse/pulse term is odd), and each jump has uniform parity across its terms.
  • Weight sector (sector::Int): tighter: H conserves total S_z ([J_z, H] = 0) and each jump is a definite-weight eigenoperator.
  • Symmetry-adapted basis (basis.sym): H and every jump are invariant under the site-permutation group, checked in addition to any coherence-order sector.

Examples

julia> check_sector_closure(restricted_basis(2, 2), zeeman(2, [1.0, 2.0])) === nothing
true

Leakage Across the Truncation Boundary

Grade projection makes the effective $\mathcal{L}$ non-normal and discards flow across the truncation boundary, the $\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{P}$ block. The leakage_operator tools assemble that block directly: a zero norm certifies exactness at the chosen cap, and a nonzero one bounds the error. This dropped flow is the physical origin of superradiance_leakage. Beyond the static cap the basis can be pruned to what the dynamics populate; see Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis.

The same P/Q split underlies effective_liouvillian, the Markovian (Schur-complement) fold of the complement into a generator on the kept sub-basis, and its non-Markovian companion memory_kernel below. The projection theory is in Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.LeakageMap — Type
LeakageMap

Precomputed handle for the grade-leakage superoperator Q 𝓛 P, the flow across the truncation boundary that grade-kmax propagation drops. Holds Lext (𝓛 on a basis extended to grade ≤ kmax+extra_grades) plus the index maps embed (retained P columns) and qrows (grade > kmax Q rows) to read off the block. Build with leakage_operator or superradiance_leakage; query with leakage_block, leakage_flow, leakage_rate.

Examples

julia> superradiance_leakage(3, 1).kmax   # the truncation grade the map is built at
1
source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.effective_liouvillian — Method
effective_liouvillian(basis, L, kept) -> (; basis, L)

The projection-operator (Nakajima-Zwanzig) effective generator on the kept sub-basis, with the complement Q adiabatically eliminated: the Schur complement

\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathcal{L}_{PP} - \mathcal{L}_{PQ}\,\mathcal{L}_{QQ}^{-1}\,\mathcal{L}_{QP},\]

where P projects onto kept and Q = 1 - P. kept is a sub-basis of basis (its labels a subset, e.g. a reachable_basis); the returned NamedTuple gives that reduced basis and the dense effective generator L on it, so propagate with propagate(res.basis, res.L, c0, ts; observables) for a c0 over the kept basis. This is the zero-frequency (Markovian) limit of the Nakajima-Zwanzig projection, exact for the stationary and slow dynamics when the eliminated Q-space is fast. It generalizes mean_field_reduce (fold a fast partner into an effective generator) to an arbitrary projector; the P-component of the full stationary state is exactly the stationary state of L.

Experimental

The eliminated block L_QQ must be invertible: the identity (trace) mode never decays, so it must be kept, and any other conserved/slow mode left in Q makes the reduction ill-defined (a near-singular L_QQ triggers a warning). This is the effective-generator (zero-frequency) limit of the full non-Markovian Nakajima-Zwanzig propagation, memory_kernel.

Examples

julia> b = restricted_basis(2, 2);

julia> L = build_liouvillian(b, hamiltonian(jcoupling(2, 1, 2, 5.0)); jumps = thermal_relaxation(2, 1:2, 0.5; p_eq = 0.3));

julia> r = effective_liouvillian(b, L, restricted_basis(2, 1));   # eliminate the grade-2 block

julia> (length(r.basis), size(r.L))
(7, (7, 7))
source

RestrictedSpinDynamics.superradiance_leakage — Method
superradiance_leakage(N, kmax; gamma=1.0, spins=1:N, extra_grades=2) -> LeakageMap

The superradiance grade-leakage superoperator ΛSR = Q Γ D[S-] P for the collective lowering jump L = S- = sum{s in spins} Is- at rate gamma, with no Hamiltonian. The two-body recycling `σi- ρ σj+` (i != j) raises grade, building the burst's pair correlations that grade-1 mean-field misses; being nonzero at every finite grade, ΛSR never vanishes under truncation the way a closing Hamiltonian's leakage does.

Examples

Memory-Kernel Reduction (Non-Markovian)

memory_kernel is the non-Markovian companion to effective_liouvillian: it propagates the full Nakajima-Zwanzig kernel, where effective_liouvillian keeps only its Markovian (s = 0) limit. Each pole of the kernel embeds as one auxiliary memory block that the ordinary propagate expv stepper advances; npoles = :all is exact, npoles = K keeps the dominant poles for a reduced-memory model. See Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism for the kernel theory.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.MemoryKernel — Type
MemoryKernel

A non-Markovian Nakajima-Zwanzig memory-kernel model on a kept sub-basis: the enlarged local generator M on the stacked [ρ_P; σ_1…σ_K; p_1…p_K]: per retained pole of Q𝓛Q, one ρ_P-driven memory block σ_j (the kernel) and one scalar inhomogeneity mode p_j (the initial-complement term). Carries the kept basis, M, the block size nP, the poles, the complement qbasis, and Uq (K x nQ, mapping ρ_Q(0) to the seeds p_j(0)). Build with memory_kernel, step with propagate; ρ_P is the first block.

Experimental

Non-normal Q𝓛Q (see memory_kernel).

source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.memory_kernel — Method
memory_kernel(basis, L, kept; npoles = :all) -> MemoryKernel

Build the (exact or pole-reduced) non-Markovian Nakajima-Zwanzig memory-kernel propagator on the kept sub-basis, eliminating the complement Q into auxiliary memory modes. The Laplace kernel K̃(s) = P𝓛Q (s - Q𝓛Q)⁻¹ Q𝓛P is rational with poles at the eigenvalues λ_j of Q𝓛Q and residues R_j = (P𝓛Q v_j)(uⱼᵀ Q𝓛P) (right v_j / left uⱼ eigenvectors), so K(τ) = Σ_j R_j e^{λ_j τ}. Each pole embeds as one auxiliary block to give the local generator

ρ̇_P = P𝓛P ρ_P + Σ_j R_j σ_j ,    σ̇_j = ρ_P + λ_j σ_j    (σ_j(0) = 0),

which propagate advances with the ordinary expv stepper. An initially populated complement (ρ_Q(0) ≠ 0) is carried by a second scalar source-free mode p_j per pole (the Nakajima-Zwanzig inhomogeneity; Breuer & Petruccione, Ch. 9), activated by passing complement0 to propagate. npoles = :all is exact (reproduces the full propagation on P for any ρ_Q(0)); npoles = K keeps the K slowest-decaying poles (closed under conjugation, so the reduced state stays real) for a reduced-memory model. The identity (trace) mode must be kept. effective_liouvillian is the single-term (s = 0) Markovian limit of this model.

Experimental

Q𝓛Q is non-normal, so its eigenvector matrix can be ill-conditioned; a large condition number triggers a warning (reduce Q, or fall back to a block-Schur / contour variant). The all-poles embedding has dimension nP·(1 + nQ), as large as the complement itself; keeping only K ≪ nQ poles is what makes it tractable.

Examples

julia> b = restricted_basis(2, 2);

julia> L = build_liouvillian(b, hamiltonian(jcoupling(2, 1, 2, 5.0)); jumps = decay(2, 1:2, 0.7));

julia> mk = memory_kernel(b, L, restricted_basis(2, 1));

julia> (length(mk.basis), mk.nP, length(mk.poles))
(7, 7, 9)
source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.propagate — Method
propagate(model::MemoryKernel, c0, ts; observables, complement0=nothing, krylov_m=30) -> (; ts, traces)

Propagate the non-Markovian MemoryKernel model from a reduced initial state c0 over the kept sub-basis, reading observables on the physical ρ_P (first block). The memory blocks start at zero; complement0 (an initial state over model.qbasis, built the same way as c0) seeds the inhomogeneity modes p_j(0) = (Uq·complement0)_j to carry an initially populated complement (ρ_Q(0) ≠ 0); omit it (default nothing) for the ρ_Q(0) = 0 case. Matrix-free Krylov expv on the enlarged local generator.

Examples

julia> b = restricted_basis(2, 2);

julia> L = build_liouvillian(b, hamiltonian(jcoupling(2, 1, 2, 5.0)); jumps = decay(2, 1:2, 0.7));

julia> kept = restricted_basis(2, 1);

julia> mk = memory_kernel(b, L, kept);

julia> res = propagate(mk, state(kept, polarized(1:2, :z)), 0.0:0.5:1.0; observables = ["Iz" => spin_op(2, 2, 3)]);

julia> length(res.ts)
3

Time-Convolutionless Reduction (TCL2)

tcl_generator is the time-convolutionless branch: instead of the memory_kernel convolution it gives a compact, time-local generator on the kept subspace alone (no pseudomode embedding). It is second order in the P/Q coupling, accurate for weak leakage, and exhibits the known TCL breakdown at strong coupling (the generator turns unstable and the propagation diverges; use memory_kernel, which is exact, there). See Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.TCLGenerator — Type
TCLGenerator

The time-convolutionless (TCL) reduction on a kept sub-basis: a time-local generator K(t) (so ρ̇_P = K(t) ρ_P), stored as the P/Q sub-blocks LPP/LPQ/LQP/LQQ plus the kept basis (observables), the block size nP, the complement qbasis (for an initial complement0), and the order. At :tcl2, K(t) = P𝓛P + P𝓛Q M(t) Q𝓛P with M(t) = (e^{Q𝓛Q t} - I)(Q𝓛Q)⁻¹; at :exact, K(t) = U̇_PP(t) U_PP(t)⁻¹ from the full block propagator (all orders in the coupling). Build with tcl_generator and step with propagate.

Experimental

See tcl_generator for the accuracy and breakdown of each order.

source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.propagate — Method
propagate(model::TCLGenerator, c0, ts; observables, complement0=nothing) -> (; ts, traces)

Propagate the time-convolutionless TCLGenerator from a reduced initial state c0 over the kept sub-basis, reading observables on ρ_P. Advances ρ̇_P = K(t) ρ_P + I(t) with a midpoint step: on each interval the frozen affine ODE is solved exactly by exponentiating the augmented generator [K(t̄) I(t̄); 0 0] (dense; nP is the small kept dimension). complement0 (an initial state over model.qbasis) supplies the TCL inhomogeneity I(t) = P𝓛Q e^{Q𝓛Q t} ρ_Q(0) for an initially populated complement; omit it (default) for ρ_Q(0) = 0.

Examples

julia> b = restricted_basis(2, 2);

julia> L = build_liouvillian(b, hamiltonian(jcoupling(2, 1, 2, 5.0)); jumps = decay(2, 1:2, 0.7));

julia> m = tcl_generator(b, L, restricted_basis(2, 1));

julia> res = propagate(m, state(m.basis, polarized(1:2, :z)), 0.0:0.5:1.0; observables = ["Iz" => spin_op(2, 2, 3)]);

julia> length(res.ts)
3
source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.tcl_generator — Method
tcl_generator(basis, L, kept; order = 2) -> TCLGenerator

Build the time-convolutionless (TCL) reduction on the kept sub-basis, eliminating the complement Q into a time-local generator K(t) (Breuer & Petruccione, Ch. 9.2). The convolutionless sibling of memory_kernel: same P/Q partition, but a compact nP x nP time-dependent generator instead of a pseudomode embedding. Both orders interpolate K(0) = P𝓛P (no leakage yet) and the Markovian limit K(∞) = P𝓛P - P𝓛Q (Q𝓛Q)⁻¹ Q𝓛P = effective_liouvillian. Step with propagate; the identity (trace) mode must be kept.

order selects the truncation in the P<->Q coupling:

  • 2 (default, recommended): K(t) = P𝓛P + P𝓛Q M(t) Q𝓛P, M(t) = (e^{Q𝓛Q t} - I)(Q𝓛Q)⁻¹, second order in the coupling. Cheap and accurate for weak leakage (a nearly grade-closed truncation). M(t) comes from a Van Loan exponential (Van Loan, IEEE Trans. Autom. Control 23, 395 (1978)), robust to a singular or non-normal Q𝓛Q.
  • :exact: the all-orders time-local generator K(t) = U̇_PP(t) U_PP(t)⁻¹ from the full block propagator U(t) = e^{t·[P𝓛P P𝓛Q; Q𝓛P Q𝓛Q]}. Exact where it exists, at the cost of one (nP+nQ) exponential per evaluation. Use it when :tcl2 is inaccurate but a time-local generator is still wanted.

Intermediate orders (TCL4, ...) are deliberately not offered: they require the ordered-cumulant multi-time integrals with no closed form past second order and are rarely worth it when :exact is available.

Experimental

Neither order is the exact dynamics that memory_kernel gives; for guaranteed-exact dynamics use that. :exact here is the exact time-local generator, which shares the genuine TCL singularity: when U_PP(t) (resp. the :tcl2 K(t)) loses invertibility at strong coupling the propagation diverges (propagate warns), so use memory_kernel there.

Examples

julia> b = restricted_basis(2, 2);

julia> L = build_liouvillian(b, hamiltonian(jcoupling(2, 1, 2, 5.0)); jumps = decay(2, 1:2, 0.7));

julia> m = tcl_generator(b, L, restricted_basis(2, 1));

julia> (length(m.basis), m.nP, size(m.LQQ, 1))
(7, 7, 9)
source

Conditional (No-Jump) Generator

Where the projection family above eliminates a subspace of 𝓛, the conditional generator removes the jump-recycling term Σ_k r_k L_k ρ L_k† instead, leaving the evolution between quantum jumps. no_jump_hamiltonian returns the non-Hermitian no-jump Hamiltonian $H_{nj} = H - \tfrac{i}{2}\sum_k r_k L_k^\dagger L_k$, the Monte Carlo wavefunction (quantum-trajectory) drift generator, whose complex eigenvalues give line positions and decay widths directly. no_jump_liouvillian assembles the matching superoperator $\mathcal{L}_{nj}\,\rho = -i(H_{nj}\rho - \rho H_{nj}^\dagger)$, whose propagated state is subnormalized with $\operatorname{Tr}\rho(t)$ equal to the no-jump survival probability.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.no_jump_hamiltonian — Method
no_jump_hamiltonian(N, H, jumps) -> PauliDict
no_jump_hamiltonian(N, H::TensorDict, jumps, radix) -> TensorDict   # higher spin

The non-Hermitian conditional (no-jump) Hamiltonian of a Lindblad model,

H_nj = H - (i/2) Σ_k r_k L_k† L_k ,

for a coherent Hamiltonian H (a PauliDict) and jumps a vector of (L::PauliDict, rate) (exactly the jumps build_liouvillian takes, e.g. from dissipator). H_nj generates the evolution conditioned on no jump occurring: the drift generator of the Monte Carlo wavefunction (quantum-trajectory) unraveling, and the operator inverted by adiabatic_elimination.

Because every Pauli string is Hermitian, L_k† is L_k with conjugated coefficients, and L_k† L_k is a Pauli-dict product (op_mult); the anti-Hermitian part -(i/2) Σ r_k L_k† L_k is added onto H. The complex eigenvalues of dense_operator(H_nj, N) give line positions (real part) and decay widths / linewidths (imaginary part) directly.

Experimental

Part of the conditional (no-jump) generator layer; not covered by the v1 stability guarantee.

Examples

julia> using LinearAlgebra: eigvals

julia> H = hamiltonian(zeeman(1, [10.0]));

julia> Hnj = no_jump_hamiltonian(1, H, decay(1, 1:1, 2.0));   # σ⁻ decay at rate 2

julia> evals = eigvals(dense_operator(Hnj, 1));

julia> round(minimum(imag, evals); digits = 6)   # excited state decays at r/2 = 1
-1.0
source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.no_jump_liouvillian — Method
no_jump_liouvillian(basis, H; jumps = [], relaxation = nothing, check_sector = true) -> SparseMatrixCSC
no_jump_liouvillian(basis, H::TensorDict; jumps = [], relaxation = nothing, check_sector = true)   # higher spin

The conditional (no-jump) Liouvillian: the restricted superoperator

𝓛_nj ρ = -i[H, ρ] - ½ Σ_k r_k {L_k† L_k, ρ} = -i(H_nj ρ - ρ H_nj†) ,

i.e. the full build_liouvillian generator with the jump-recycling term Σ_k r_k L_k ρ L_k† removed (H_nj is the no_jump_hamiltonian). It propagates the subnormalized conditional state between quantum jumps; tr ρ(t) decays from 1 as the no-jump survival probability. Same calling convention and grade projection as build_liouvillian; feed the result to propagate. Pauli representation (the workhorse) and the higher-spin tensor representation; the ladder representation is not covered.

Experimental

Part of the conditional (no-jump) generator layer; not covered by the v1 stability guarantee.

Examples

julia> basis = restricted_basis(1, 1);

julia> L = no_jump_liouvillian(basis, PauliDict(); jumps = decay(1, 1:1, 2.0));

julia> size(L)
(4, 4)

Adiabatic Elimination

Adiabatic elimination folds a fast-decaying subspace out of the dynamics by slaving the fast modes to the slow ones. adiabatic_elimination offers both forms of the same Hilbert manifold fold (Reiter & Sorensen

  1. through its space keyword. space = :hilbert (the default) inverts the no-jump Hamiltonian $H_{nj}$

on the excited manifold and returns a reduced Lindblad model (; hamiltonian, jumps), 2nd-order in the coupling, manifestly GKSL, and composable back into build_liouvillian. space = :liouville returns the exact-Markovian Schur fold of the manifold superprojector $P[\rho] = \Pi_g\,\rho\,\Pi_g$ as a dense superoperator (; liouvillian, ops), all orders in the coupling but generally not GKSL and requiring the whole excited block $\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{Q}$ to be invertible. The two agree to 2nd order.

effective_liouvillian above is the related Liouville-space fold by correlation grade in the sparse Pauli basis, rather than by Hilbert manifold. Reach for adiabatic_elimination when the eliminated subspace is a weakly populated fast manifold, and for effective_liouvillian when it is a set of high-correlation grade shells.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.adiabatic_elimination — Function
adiabatic_elimination(N, H, jumps, ground; space = :hilbert, tol = 1e-10)
adiabatic_elimination(N, H::TensorDict, jumps, ground, radix; …)        # higher spin

Adiabatic elimination of a fast-decaying excited Hilbert manifold, folding its influence onto the ground manifold (Reiter & Sorensen, Phys. Rev. A 85, 032111 (2012)). ground is the ground-manifold projector (a 2^N x 2^N, or prod(radix) x prod(radix) for a tensor basis, Hermitian idempotent) or, via the convenience method, a vector of ground computational-basis indices; the excited manifold is its complement. For a higher-spin basis pass the per-site dimensions as the trailing radix positional. Valid when the excited manifold is weakly populated and fast (‖V‖ small against its gap/decay); dense (small N), like the AHT layer. space picks the output:

  • :hilbert (default) returns a reduced Lindblad model (; hamiltonian, jumps) on the ground manifold that plugs straight back into build_liouvillian. Splitting H by the projector into within-manifold blocks H_g, H_e and perturbative couplings V₊ (ground→excited), V₋ = V₊†, and writing H_nj = no_jump_hamiltonian restricted to the excited manifold,

    H_eff   = -½ V₋ (H_nj⁻¹ + H_nj⁻†) V₊ + H_g
L_eff^k = √r_k L_k H_nj⁻¹ V₊            (plus any purely-ground block of L_k, carried over)

    2nd-order in the coupling and manifestly GKSL. Needs only the excited block of H_nj invertible.

  • :liouville returns (; liouvillian, ops): the dense effective superoperator from the exact-Markovian Schur fold of the manifold superprojector P[ρ] = Π_g ρ Π_g, 𝓛_eff = P𝓛P - P𝓛Q (Q𝓛Q)⁻¹ Q𝓛P. It acts on vec(σ) for a d_g × d_g reduced density matrix σ in the ground orthonormal basis ops (columns), reconstructed as ops * σ * ops'. All orders in the coupling but generally not GKSL, and it needs the whole excited Liouville block Q𝓛Q invertible (so a decoupled, undamped mode in the excited manifold makes it singular, unlike :hilbert).

The two agree to 2nd order. This is adiabatic elimination by Hilbert manifold; effective_liouvillian is the related Liouville-space fold by correlation grade in the sparse Pauli basis.

Experimental

One of the effective-generator reductions (with effective_liouvillian); not covered by the v1 stability guarantee.

Examples

julia> H = hamiltonian(zeeman(2, [0.0, 80.0]), drive_field(2, 2; ω1 = 5.0));  # spin 2 split high + weak drive

julia> jumps = decay(2, 2:2, 40.0);                                           # fast decay of spin 2

julia> red = adiabatic_elimination(2, H, jumps, [2, 4]);                      # keep the spin-2-down states

julia> red.hamiltonian isa PauliDict && red.jumps isa Vector
true

julia> L = adiabatic_elimination(2, H, jumps, [2, 4]; space = :liouville);    # Liouville-space form

julia> size(L.liouvillian)                                                    # d_g² × d_g², d_g = 2
