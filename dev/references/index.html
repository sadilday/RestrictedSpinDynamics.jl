References

An annotated bibliography of the theory this package builds on. Derivations that used to live here (the grade-basis size, the DLA truncation floor, the relaxation reduction ladder) have moved into the guides (Grade Truncation and Polynomial State Space Scaling, Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism, and Relaxation Backends) and are cross-referenced below.

Restricted State Space and Truncation

Kuprov, Wagner-Rundell, Hore, "Polynomially scaling spin dynamics simulation algorithm based on adaptive state-space restriction," J. Magn. Reson. 189, 241 (2007). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmr.2007.09.014. The origin of the restricted-state-space idea this package implements: keep only low-order product operators and the cost scales polynomially instead of as 4^N. The Spinach lineage. Their exactness axiom is ours: the retained set must be closed under the generator (their "loose ends" leaving the space are the boundary block Q L P of Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis).

Kuprov, "Polynomially scaling spin dynamics II: further state-space compression using Krylov subspace techniques and zero track elimination," J. Magn. Reson. 195, 45 (2008). https://spindynamics.org/publications/016kuprov2008.pdf. Zero track elimination (ZTE): probe a trajectory, drop density-matrix rows ("tracks") that stay near zero, and propagate on the reduced Liouvillian. The direct ancestor of zero_track_elimination. The observation that the trajectory lives in the Krylov subspace span{rho0, L rho0, L^2 rho0, ...} is what reachable_basis enumerates symbolically on the coupling graph instead of numerically by Lanczos.

Krzystyniak, Edwards, Kuprov, "Destination state screening of active spaces in spin dynamics simulations," J. Magn. Reson. 210, 228 (2011). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmr.2011.01.005. Screens the active space by which states connect backward to the detected (destination) states, the observable-informed pruning behind direction = :observable and the criterion = :dual importance weight in zero_track_elimination.

Karabanov, Kuprov, Charnock, van der Drift, Edwards, Kockenberger, "On the accuracy of the state space restriction approximation for spin dynamics simulations," J. Chem. Phys. 135, 084106 (2011). https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3866. The accuracy theory for SSR: correlation order climbs the ladder at a bounded speed set by the coupling ||H_2|| and is drained by relaxation ||R_1||, so leakage past a cutoff grade is bounded by the ratio ||H_2|| / ||R_1||. Their per-order norm |rho_k|^2 is the statistical counterpart of the trajectory-weighted flow meter in Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis; the exact algebraic version is the dla_report grade floor.

Kuprov, Spin: From Basic Symmetries to Quantum Optimal Control (Springer, 2023). https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-05607-9. Frames spin dynamics in controllability / reachability / observability terms and develops GRAPE optimal control, the same system-theoretic language used here for basis selection in Reachability, Control, and the Adaptive Basis, applied there to pulse design.

Wiersema, Kokcu, Kemper, Bakalov, "Classification of dynamical Lie algebras of 2-local spin systems on linear, circular and fully connected topologies," npj Quantum Information 10, 110 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-024-00900-2. The formal backing for the truncation floor: grade truncation is exact iff the dynamical Lie algebra closes within the kept grades, and the DLA (hence the required grade) is set by the coupling topology. See Grade Truncation and Polynomial State Space Scaling.

Kokcu, Wiersema, Kemper, Bakalov, "Classification of dynamical Lie algebras generated by spin interactions on undirected graphs," J. Math. Phys. 67, 052205 (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19797. Extends the DLA classification from chains, rings, and complete graphs to arbitrary undirected coupling graphs: beyond one dimension the DLA depends only on whether the graph is bipartite, and a polynomially-sized DLA (hence a low, spin-count-independent dla_report grade floor) is special to 1D. The topological answer to when grade truncation stays cheap: chain-like coupling closes at low grade, while dense or higher-dimensional graphs generically do not. See Grade Truncation and Polynomial State Space Scaling.

Open-System Relaxation Theory

Kubo, "Stochastic Liouville Equations," J. Math. Phys. 4, 174 (1963); Anderson, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 9, 316 (1954). The exact classical-bath route implemented by sle / propagate_sle: a Hamiltonian fluctuating over a classical M-state bath, valid at any field and through level crossings. The top of the reduction ladder in Relaxation Backends.

Gorini, Kossakowski, Sudarshan, J. Math. Phys. 17, 821 (1976); Lindblad, Commun. Math. Phys. 48, 119 (1976). The general completely-positive (GKSL) generator with a PSD Kossakowski matrix. Off-diagonal entries are cross-correlated relaxation (CSA x dipolar, the basis of TROSY and a driver of singlet-order leakage in the Pileio-Levitt long-lived-states theory); assembled here by kossakowski_matrix and dissipators_from_kossakowski.

Davidovic, "Completely Positive, Simple, and Possibly Highly Accurate Approximation of the Redfield Equation," Quantum 4, 326 (2020). https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2020-09-21-326. The geometric-mean master equation restores complete positivity to Bloch-Redfield by replacing an arithmetic mean of decay rates with a geometric one, keeping non-secular accuracy. The method = :davidovic path of redfield_jumps.

Nathan, Rudner, "Universal Lindblad equation for open quantum systems," Phys. Rev. B 102, 115109 (2020). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.102.115109. Recasts Bloch-Redfield into Lindblad form with jump operators filtered by sqrt(g(omega)), completely positive by construction and handling quasi-degeneracy. The method = :nathan_rudner path.

Levitt, Di Bari, "Steady state in magnetic resonance pulse experiments," Phys. Rev. Lett. 69, 3124 (1992). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.69.3124 (and the follow-up "The homogeneous master equation and the manipulation of relaxation networks," Bull. Magn. Reson., 1994). The homogeneous master equation: a single inhomogeneous term drives relaxation toward thermal equilibrium, the detailed-balance foundation the later thermalized Lindblad forms build on.

Bengs, Levitt, "A master equation for spin systems far from equilibrium," J. Magn. Reson. 310, 106645 (2020). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmr.2019.106645. The thermalized Lindblad ("Lb") master equation: the secular GKSL dissipator with the classical spectral density weighted by the Schofield / KMS factor exp(-beta*omega/2), giving a completely positive generator that relaxes to the Boltzmann state even far from equilibrium (unlike the inhomogeneous master equation). The basis for bengs_levitt_jumps, davies_jumps, and the thermalize flag of the Zeeman-secular backends.

Schofield, "Space-Time Correlation Function Formalism for Slow Neutron Scattering," Phys. Rev. Lett. 4, 239 (1960). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.4.239. Origin of the quantum detailed-balance (Schofield) correction exp(-beta*omega/2) that asymmetrizes the classical spectral density so the thermalized generators above relax to the Boltzmann state rather than infinite temperature.

Schaller, Brandes, "Preservation of positivity by dynamical coarse graining," Phys. Rev. A 78, 022106 (2008); Trushechkin, "Unified GKLS quantum master equation beyond the secular approximation," Phys. Rev. A 103, 062226 (2021). Two further CP-preserving, non-secular reconstructions that interpolate between Redfield and the secular limit, context for the CP-vs-accuracy tradeoff discussed in Relaxation Backends.

Paramagnetic and Second-Kind Relaxation

Abragam, The Principles of Nuclear Magnetism (Oxford, 1961), ch. VIII; Solomon, Phys. Rev. 99, 559 (1955); Bloembergen, J. Chem. Phys. 27, 572 (1957). Relaxation of the first and second kind, and the dipolar cross/auto (Solomon) rates. The second-kind picture, a fast-relaxing partner eliminated into a fluctuating field, is ScalarSecondKind / DipolarSecondKind, and the solomon_rates the first-kind dipolar backend reproduces.

Hwang, Freed, "Dynamic effects of pair correlation functions on spin relaxation by translational diffusion in liquids," J. Chem. Phys. 63, 4017 (1975); Freed, ibid. 68, 4034 (1978); Ayant, Belorizky, Fries, Rosset, J. Physique 36, 991 (1975). The force-free translational spectral density for a freely diffusing partner, with its characteristic sqrt(omega) low-frequency dispersion, implemented as Translational and the physics behind the outer-sphere paramagnetic channel.

Caravan, Ellison, McMurry, Lauffer, "Gadolinium(III) chelates as MRI contrast agents," Chem. Rev. 99, 2293 (1999). The outer-sphere relaxivity lineage; outer_sphere_second_kind / outer_sphere_r1 package the freely-diffusing case as a per-mM relaxivity, validated against the TEMPOL nitroxide NMRD profile across roughly eight decades of field.

SABRE and Chemical Exchange

Barskiy, Knecht, Yurkovskaya, Ivanov, "SABRE: Chemical kinetics and spin dynamics of the formation of hyperpolarization," Prog. Nucl. Magn. Reson. Spectrosc. 114-115, 33 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnmrs.2019.05.005. The analytical model behind the SABRE pump channels: the exchange reset of the parahydrogen hydride pair to the fresh singlet (singlet_refresh, Eq. 72) and the reduced exchange-vs-transfer pump rate (sabre_rate, Eq. 68, peaking at k_d = ω_LAC).

Markelov, Snadin, Kiryutin, Barskiy, Yurkovskaya, "Scalable modeling of multi-spin ensembles in SABRE hyperpolarization: a symmetry-based framework for zero and ultralow fields," Magn. Reson. 7, 53 (2026). https://doi.org/10.5194/mr-7-53-2026. A contemporary symmetry-restricted approach to the problem this package targets: at zero and ultralow field the Hamiltonian, relaxation, and exchange superoperators share a total-spin symmetry, so the dynamics restrict rigorously to the zero-quantum coherence subspace. That yields a scalable multi-spin SABRE simulation, validated against full Liouville space and applied to a 14-spin complex. The zero-quantum restriction is the coherence-order (weight) ladder sector here (see Basis & Symmetry); the exchange reset is singlet_refresh / sabre.

Projection Methods and Reduced Dynamics

Nakajima, Prog. Theor. Phys. 20, 948 (1958); Zwanzig, J. Chem. Phys. 33, 1338 (1960); Breuer, Petruccione, The Theory of Open Quantum Systems (Oxford, 2002), Ch. 9. The projection-operator formalism behind the grade reductions: the Nakajima-Zwanzig memory kernel (memory_kernel) and its Markovian Schur-complement limit (effective_liouvillian), the time-convolutionless generator (tcl_generator), and the initial inhomogeneity for a populated complement. See Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism.

Van Loan, "Computing integrals involving the matrix exponential," IEEE Trans. Autom. Control 23, 395 (1978). https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1978.1101743. The block-triangular ("augmented") exponential that evaluates the time-convolutionless M(t) = (e^{Q𝓛Q t} - I)(Q𝓛Q)⁻¹ in a single exp without inverting Q𝓛Q, the numerical engine behind tcl_generator.

Shabani, Lidar, "Completely positive post-Markovian master equation via a measurement approach," Phys. Rev. A 71, 020101(R) (2005). The post-Markovian master equation (pome): the Markovian generator convolved with a phenomenological exponential memory kernel, embedded as a local generator on the state plus one auxiliary memory mode.

Symmetry Adaptation

Dixon, "High speed computation of group characters," Numer. Math. 10, 446 (1967). https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02162877. The multiplication-table route to irreducible characters behind character_table: the class sums' structure-constant matrices commute, so their common eigenvectors carry the central characters, no cycle-type class key or stored irrep tables needed. Feeds the multi-dimensional-irrep analysis (irrep_multiplicities, symmetry_report).

Non-normality and Transient Growth

Trefethen, Embree, Spectra and Pseudospectra: The Behavior of Nonnormal Matrices and Operators (Princeton, 2005); Kreiss, "Ueber die Stabilitaetsdefinition fuer Differenzengleichungen die partielle Differentialgleichungen approximieren," BIT 2, 153 (1962). Because the restricted Liouvillian is non-normal, its spectral abscissa governs only the long-time rate while the norm of exp(tL) can amplify transiently first. The Spectral (Contour) transient-growth tools rest on this theory: the numerical abscissa (RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.numerical_abscissa), the Kreiss constant and its two-sided bracket (RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.kreiss_bracket), the measured envelope (RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.transient_bound), and the optimal-growth initial state (RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.optimal_growth).

Petermann, "Calculated spontaneous emission factor for double-heterostructure injection lasers with gain-induced waveguiding," IEEE J. Quantum Electron. 15, 566 (1979). https://doi.org/10.1109/JQE.1979.1070064; Siegman, "Excess spontaneous emission in non-Hermitian optical systems. I. Laser amplifiers," Phys. Rev. A 39, 1253 (1989). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.39.1253. The excess-noise (Petermann) factor: the norm of the non-normal spectral projector, equal to the squared eigenvalue condition number for a simple eigenvalue and diverging at an exceptional point. Reported as the operator norm ‖P‖₂² (not the Frobenius norm, which differ for a degenerate cluster) by RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.petermann_factor and RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.eigstructure.
