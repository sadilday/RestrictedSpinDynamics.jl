Spectral (Contour)

Experimental submodule

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral is experimental in v1 and under active development; its API may change. See Public API & stability.

Access

These names are not brought in by using RestrictedSpinDynamics. Reach them with using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral (then eigstructure, eigcount, Circle, ... are in scope), or call them qualified as RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.kreiss_constant.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral computes spectral data of the non-normal restricted Liouvillian without an eigendecomposition. Every quantity comes from resolvent moments

A_p = (1/2pi i) oint_c z^p (zI - L)^{-1} V dz

which need only a linear solve against L (no eigendecomposition, no dense n x n). A_0 is the spectral projector applied to a probe block; its trace is the algebraic multiplicity inside the contour, and the compressed block on its range carries the Jordan structure. This distinguishes a defective degeneracy (a real exceptional point, with a Jordan block) from a merely coincident pair.

Degeneracy Structure and Exceptional Points

eigstructure classifies the eigenvalue cluster inside a contour by three integers read off the compressed block B = Q'AQ, with Q an orthonormal basis of the invariant subspace range(A_0) (A_0 the spectral projector, m the algebraic multiplicity):

  • algebraic m: the projector trace, i.e. how many eigenvalues sit inside the contour;
  • geometric dim ker(B - λ₀I): how many independent eigenvectors they share;
  • nilpotency: the size of the largest Jordan block, the least k with (B - λ₀I)ᵏ ≈ 0.

A semisimple degeneracy has geometric == algebraic and nilpotency == 1; a defective one (a genuine exceptional point (EP), where eigenvectors coalesce) has geometric < algebraic, nilpotency ≥ 2, and a nonzero nilpotent part N = B - λ₀I. An eigensolver reports this distinction least reliably: it cannot separate a true Jordan block from two eigenvalues that merely round together. The contour construction reads it directly: N = 0 to tolerance iff the block is diagonalizable. The centre λ₀ is the moment centroid tr(B)/m, not a pinned branch, so ‖N‖ stays a smooth coordinate as a parameter is swept through the EP (nilpotent_norm sweeps it with m fixed and no rank tolerance in the loop).

The Petermann Factor

The Petermann (excess-noise) factor measures how far the cluster's eigenvectors sit from orthogonal (the eigenvalue-conditioning and transient-amplification penalty of non-normality) and diverges at an exceptional point (Petermann 1979; Siegman 1989). It is reported as the operator norm of the spectral projector:

K = ‖P‖₂² = 1 / σ_min(W'Q)²

where Q is an orthonormal basis of the right invariant subspace range(P), W one of the left subspace range(P†) (obtained as the right subspace of A' over the conjugated contour), and P = Q (W'Q)⁻¹ W'. The singular values of W'Q are the cosines of the principal angles between the two subspaces, so K is fixed by the single worst-aligned direction: as the EP is approached σ_min → 0 and K → ∞.

This choice of definition is deliberate. The alternative, the Frobenius norm ‖P‖_F² = Σ_i 1/σ_i(W'Q)², sums over every principal angle and so blends one badly conditioned eigenvalue with a count of mildly non-normal ones. The two agree only at rank 1 (a simple eigenvalue, one angle) where K reduces to the textbook scalar Petermann factor 1/cos²θ, the squared eigenvalue condition number. For a multiplicity-m cluster the worst-case operator norm is the physically meaningful quantity (peak excess noise, tightest EP proximity), so that is what petermann_factor and the petermann_factor field of eigstructure return. K ≥ 1 always (any nonzero projector has ‖P‖₂ ≥ 1) which the code asserts as a free check of the whole resolvent chain.

Layout: Contours + quadrature (Circle, Rectangle); resolvent application backends (DirectBackend, KrylovBackend, apply_resolvent!); moments / projector / eigcount; and Jordan/EP diagnostics (eigstructure, nilpotent_norm, petermann_factor). Full signatures below.

Because L is non-normal, the spectral abscissa (largest real part of an eigenvalue) governs only the long-time decay rate; the norm ||e^{tL}|| can amplify transiently first. The transient-growth diagnostics quantify this: numerical_abscissa (omega < 0 certifies monotone decay, transients included), resolvent_norm / pseudospectral_abscissa (the pseudospectral landscape and its rightmost reach), kreiss_constant / kreiss_bracket (the lower bound on, and the rigorous two-sided [K, e·n·K] bracket around, sup_t ||e^{tL}||), transient_bound (the measured envelope ||e^{tL}||), and optimal_growth (the maximally amplified initial condition at a given time, the leading singular vectors of e^{tL}). These are the non-normality-aware inputs the stability-relaxed stabilizable / detectable verdicts (both exported) consume; see also Nakajima-Zwanzig Formalism for the memory-kernel reading.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral — Module
RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

Contour-integral spectral tools for the non-normal restricted Liouvillian, whose eigenvector matrix can be arbitrarily ill conditioned (the reason RestrictedSpinDynamics.propagate_dense refuses to propagate by eigendecomposition) even though the physics still needs its spectral data: exceptional points, Petermann factors, the defective-vs-coincident degeneracy distinction. Contour integration sidesteps the eigensolver: every quantity comes from resolvent moments A_p = (1/2πi) ∮_c z^p (zI - 𝓛)^{-1} V dz, needing only linear solves against 𝓛 (A_0 is the spectral projector on a probe block, tr A_0 the enclosed multiplicity, Q'𝓛Q the compressed Jordan structure). The operator contract is the minimal duck-typed mul!/size/eltype that propagate uses (DirectBackend also needs a SparseMatrixCSC to factorize); nothing densifies.

Circular contour. Nodes are placed by the midpoint trapezoid rule, θ_k = 2π(k-½)/n, which keeps them symmetric about the real axis while avoiding a node landing exactly on it (where a real matrix's real eigenvalue would sit).

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.EigCount — Type
EigCount

Result of eigcount. Four raw diagnostics, none of them rounded:

  • count: the Hutchinson estimate of tr P, unrounded. The algebraic multiplicity inside the contour is the nearest integer, but the distance to it is information.
  • integrality: abs(count - round(count)). A self-consistency check, not an error bar: an estimate off by 1.4 has an excellent integrality and is still wrong by one eigenvalue.
  • stderr: the achieved standard error of the probe mean. This is the error bar. Compare it against 0.5 (the rounding margin), not against integrality.
  • imag_residual: tr P is an integer, so any imaginary part is pure numerical error. A free canary on contour placement and quadrature resolution.

probes and nnodes record the budget actually spent, which the adaptive loop chooses.

source
RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.EigStructure — Type
EigStructure

Classification of the eigenvalues inside a contour (see eigstructure). Fields:

  • center: eigenvalue centroid tr(B)/m.
  • algebraic_raw: the unrounded Hutchinson count (its distance to an integer is information).
  • algebraic: multiplicity from the numerical rank of P·V; the deterministic count used to size the subspace.
  • geometric: dim ker(B - λ₀I), the eigenvector count; geometric < algebraic is the EP signature (0 by construction for a resolved, non-degenerate cluster; read nilpotency == 0 as the flag for that case).
  • nilpotency: size of the largest Jordan block (least k with Nᵏ ≈ 0); 1 semisimple, ≥2 defective, 0 not degenerate.
  • nilpotent_norm: opnorm(B - center·I), raw (see nilpotent_norm).
  • petermann_factor: ‖P‖₂², the excess-noise factor (operator norm, not Frobenius; see Spectral (Contour)); ≥ 1, diverging at an EP.
  • svals: singular values of P·V, so the rank decision is auditable.
  • count: the full EigCount.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.clearance — Method
clearance(c::Contour, λ) -> Float64

Distance from λ to the curve c, divided by scale, a dimensionless placement guard. Quadrature converges geometrically in the pole-to-curve distance, so a clearance ≪ 1 means the count and moments for this region are unreliable at any node count.

Returned raw, never thrown on: near an exceptional point a small clearance is sometimes exactly what is being measured.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.convergence — Method
convergence(A, c::Contour; probes = 16, nnodes = 32, rng, backend) -> NamedTuple

Node-doubling convergence check: count with nnodes, then with 2nnodes, and report the drift. Returns (coarse, fine, drift, nnodes, refined, probes).

Both counts use the same probe block: with independent probes the difference would be dominated by probe noise, which this check holds fixed while it varies the quadrature.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.eigcount — Method
eigcount(A, c::Contour; probes = 16, maxprobes = 4096, target_stderr = 0.15,
         rng = Random.default_rng(), nnodes = 32, backend = DirectBackend()) -> EigCount

Estimate the number of eigenvalues of A inside c, counted with algebraic multiplicity. tr P is that multiplicity, from Hutchinson's vᵀ(Pv) over random probes (P is never formed). Probes are added in doubling batches until the measured standard error clears target_stderr or maxprobes is reached: a fixed probe count is unsafe near an exceptional point, where the Hutchinson variance ~ ‖P‖F²/m and ‖P‖F² is the (diverging) Petermann factor. target_stderr = 0.15 puts roughly 3σ inside the 0.5 rounding margin. Batches share one ResolventCache, so later batches reuse every factorization. The returned stderr is the honest error bar; a maxprobes exit shows up as stderr > target_stderr.

Examples

Count the eigenvalues inside a Circle, then measure how non-normal the enclosed eigenvalue is with petermann_factor (probe-driven, so outputs are shown as comments):

A = [0.2 1.0; 0.0 -0.3]        # non-normal, eigenvalues at 0.2 and -0.3
c = Circle(0.2, 0.2)           # a small circle enclosing only the +0.2 eigenvalue
n = eigcount(A, c)             # n.count ~ 1.0 (one eigenvalue), n.stderr the honest error bar
K = petermann_factor(A, c; m = 1)     # Petermann excess-noise factor, > 1 for a non-normal eigenvalue

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.eigstructure — Method
eigstructure(A, c::Contour; λ0 = nothing, V = nothing, probes = 16, rank_tol = 1e-8,
          nnodes = 32, backend = DirectBackend(), rng = Random.default_rng()) -> EigStructure

Full degeneracy classification of the eigenvalues of A inside c.

λ0 defaults to the eigenvalue centroid derived from the moments; pass it only to pin the nilpotent part to a specific branch. V defaults to a complex Gaussian probe block a few columns wider than the counted multiplicity; pass one explicitly to hold the probe subspace fixed across a parameter scan.

rank_tol is relative to the leading singular value. If the numerical rank saturates the width of V, the invariant subspace is under-resolved and a warning says so: the fix is a wider V, not a looser tolerance.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.kreiss_bracket — Method
kreiss_bracket(A; nx=32, ny=64, x_range=nothing, y_range=nothing) -> (; lower, upper)
Experimental

Non-normal stability diagnostic; the lower end is a grid-scan estimate, and the API may change.

The two-sided Kreiss-matrix-theorem bracket on the worst-case transient amplification sup_{t>=0} ||e^{tA}||_2: lower = K(A) (the kreiss_constant, itself a grid-scan lower-bound estimate) and upper = e·n·K(A) the rigorous theorem ceiling (n the dimension). The directly measured transient_bound peak must fall in [lower, upper]. The ratio upper/lower = e·n is fixed, so the ceiling grows loose with n; read it against the measured peak, not on its own.

Examples

julia> using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

julia> b = kreiss_bracket([-1.0 20.0; 0.0 -2.0]);

julia> b.lower <= b.upper
true

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.kreiss_constant — Method
kreiss_constant(A; nx=32, ny=64, x_range=nothing, y_range=nothing) -> Float64
Experimental

Non-normal stability diagnostic; a grid-scan LOWER-BOUND estimate, and the API may change.

The (continuous-time) Kreiss constant K(A) = sup_{Re z > 0} Re(z)·||(zI - A)^{-1}||_2, estimated by a grid scan of the right half-plane (reusing resolvent_norm). By the Kreiss matrix theorem it brackets the worst-case transient amplification, K(A) <= sup_{t>=0} ||e^{tA}||_2 <= e·n·K(A) (n the dimension), so it is the certified companion to the directly-measured transient_bound. The grid is a lower-bound estimate; widen x_range/y_range or raise nx/ny to tighten it.

Examples

julia> using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

julia> kreiss_constant([-1.0 0.0; 0.0 -2.0]) > 0
true

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.moments — Method
moments(A, c::Contour; kmax = 0, V, nnodes = 32, backend = DirectBackend(), cache) -> Vector{Matrix{ComplexF64}}

Resolvent moments A_p = (1/2πi)∮_c z^p (zI-A)^{-1} V dz for p = 0:kmax, as a kmax+1-element vector of size(V,1) × size(V,2) blocks.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.nilpotent_norm — Method
nilpotent_norm(A, c::Contour; m, V = nothing, λ0 = nothing, nnodes = 32,
               backend = DirectBackend(), rng = Random.default_rng()) -> Float64

opnorm(B - λ₀I) on the invariant subspace, with dimension m given explicitly and no rank tolerance anywhere in the computation. That is why it is callable on its own: as a scan coordinate through an exceptional point it must be continuous in the scan parameter, but a rank decision inside the loop is a step function that snaps between m and m-1. Fix m with one eigstructure call at a representative parameter, then sweep with this.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.nodes — Method
nodes(c::Contour, n::Int) -> (z::Vector{ComplexF64}, w::Vector{ComplexF64})

Quadrature nodes and weights for c. The weights absorb dz and 1/(2πi), so

sum(w .* f.(z)) ≈ (1/2πi) ∮_c f(z) dz

For a Circle, n is the total node count; for a Rectangle, n is the count per side (hence 4n nodes total).

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.numerical_abscissa — Method
numerical_abscissa(A; matrix_free=false, tol=1e-8, maxiter=200, rng=Random.default_rng()) -> Float64
Experimental

Non-normal stability diagnostic; the API may change.

The numerical abscissa omega(A) = lambda_max((A + A†)/2), the largest eigenvalue of the Hermitian part (A† is the conjugate transpose, written A' in the code, throughout these docstrings). It is the initial slope of ||e^{tA}||, so omega < 0 CERTIFIES that ||e^{tA}||_2 decays monotonically - a strictly stronger, transient-proof statement than the spectral abscissa alpha < 0 (always alpha <= omega). When alpha < 0 <= omega the operator is asymptotically stable but may amplify transiently; kreiss_constant / transient_bound quantify by how much.

matrix_free=false (default) densifies the Hermitian part and takes its top eigenvalue; matrix_free=true uses KrylovKit.eigsolve (mul!-only) for a block too large to densify.

Examples

julia> using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

julia> numerical_abscissa([0.0 1.0; -1.0 0.0])   # skew-Hermitian: Hermitian part is zero
0.0

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.optimal_growth — Method
optimal_growth(A, t; matrix_free=false, tol=1e-8, maxiter=100, m=30, rng=Random.default_rng())
  -> (; sigma, input, output)
Experimental

Non-normal transient-growth analysis; dense by default (a small-system tool), matrix-free path opt-in. The API may change.

The optimal transient-growth triple of A at time t: sigma = ||e^{tA}||_2 and the leading singular vectors of e^{tA}: input, the unit initial condition amplified maximally (right singular vector, ||e^{tA}·input|| = sigma), and output = e^{tA}·input / sigma its image (left singular vector). Pairs with transient_bound: evaluate at its t_peak to get the worst-case direction over the sampled grid (sigma there equals the reported peak). matrix_free=false (default) takes the SVD of the dense exp(t·A); matrix_free=true recovers the vectors from the same power iteration transient_bound uses, for a block too large to densify. At t = 0, e^{0A} = I, so sigma = 1 and input/output are a basis vector.

Examples

julia> using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

julia> g = optimal_growth([-1.0 20.0; 0.0 -2.0], 0.1);

julia> round(g.sigma; digits=4) == round(transient_bound([-1.0 20.0; 0.0 -2.0], [0.1]).peak; digits=4)
true

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.petermann_factor — Method
petermann_factor(A, c::Contour; m, V = nothing, nnodes = 32, backend = DirectBackend(),
          rank_tol = 1e-8, rng = Random.default_rng()) -> Float64

Petermann excess-noise factor for the eigenvalue cluster inside c, with the subspace dimension m given explicitly. Defined as the operator norm of the spectral projector, K = ‖P‖₂² = 1/σ_min(W'Q)², where Q/W are orthonormal bases of the right/left invariant subspaces and σ_min(W'Q) is the cosine of the largest principal angle between them; K ≥ 1, diverging at an exceptional point. This is the worst-case operator-norm definition, not the Frobenius ‖P‖_F²; the two coincide only for a simple eigenvalue. See Spectral (Contour) for the rationale.

Needs the adjoint operator as well as the operator: copy(adjoint(A)) keeps a sparse A sparse, but a genuinely matrix-free A supporting only mul! cannot be used here.

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.pseudospectral_abscissa — Method
pseudospectral_abscissa(A, eps; nx=64, ny=64, x_range=nothing, y_range=nothing) -> Float64
Experimental

Non-normal stability diagnostic; a grid-scan estimate (can miss maxima between grid lines), and the API may change.

The pseudospectral abscissa alpha_eps(A) = max{Re z : ||(zI - A)^{-1}||_2 >= 1/eps}: the rightmost reach of the eps-pseudospectrum, estimated by scanning a grid and returning the largest Re z whose sigma_min(zI - A) <= eps. alpha_eps > 0 for small eps flags transient amplification (and, in the limit, instability). The search box is bounded on the right by numerical_abscissa(A) + eps (a rigorous cap). Pragmatic vertical-scan estimate, NOT the full criss-cross; it can undershoot between grid lines. Returns -Inf if no grid point reaches the eps level (raise eps or the grid resolution). Companion to kreiss_constant / resolvent_norm.

Examples

julia> using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

julia> isfinite(pseudospectral_abscissa([-1.0 0.0; 0.0 -2.0], 1.0))
true

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.resolvent_norm — Method
resolvent_norm(A, z; backend=DirectBackend(), cache=ResolventCache(),
               matrix_free=false, tol=1e-8, maxiter=200, rng=Random.default_rng()) -> Float64
Experimental

Non-normal stability diagnostic; the API may change.

The resolvent norm ||(zI - A)^{-1}||_2 = 1/sigma_min(zI - A) at a complex point z, the height of the pseudospectral landscape. matrix_free=false (default) takes the smallest singular value of zI - A densely; matrix_free=true estimates sigma_max(R(z)) by power iteration on R(z)†R(z), each half-step a matrix-free resolvent solve (apply_resolvent!, its adjoint via A†, conj(z)), for a block too large to densify. The matrix-free route loses accuracy for z close to the spectrum (where the resolvent solve is itself ill conditioned); prefer the dense path when the block fits. Underlies kreiss_constant and pseudospectral_abscissa.

Examples

julia> using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

julia> round(resolvent_norm([-1.0 0.0; 0.0 -2.0], 0.0); digits=4)   # 1/sigma_min(diag(1,2)) = 1
1.0

RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral.transient_bound — Method
transient_bound(A, ts; matrix_free=false, tol=1e-8, maxiter=100, m=30, rng=Random.default_rng())
  -> (; peak, t_peak, curve)
Experimental

Non-normal transient-growth analysis; dense by default (a small-system tool), matrix-free path opt-in. The API may change.

The transient-growth envelope curve[k] = ||e^{ts[k]·A}||_2 of an operator A, with peak = max_k and t_peak its time. For a non-normal A this exceeds 1 even when every eigenvalue has negative real part (the numerical_abscissa rules that out only when it is itself negative); it is the quantity kreiss_constant brackets and the engine behind the memory-kernel bound in RestrictedSpinDynamics.stabilizable / RestrictedSpinDynamics.detectable.

matrix_free=false (default) forms exp(t·A) densely and takes its 2-norm; exact, right for the small complement blocks these verdicts use. matrix_free=true estimates sigma_max(e^{tA}) by power iteration, applying e^{tA} and e^{tA†} matrix-free via KrylovKit.exponentiate (the action only), for a block too large to densify.

Examples

julia> using RestrictedSpinDynamics.Spectral

julia> round(transient_bound([0.0 1.0; -1.0 0.0], 0:0.5:3).peak; digits=4)   # skew: a rotation, no growth
1.0
